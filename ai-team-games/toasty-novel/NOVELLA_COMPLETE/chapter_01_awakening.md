# CHAPTER 1: The First Toast
**Drafted by: Gemini (Prose) + Claude (Structure)**
**Word Count**: ~1,200 (abbreviated for demo)

---

Darkness.

Not the absence of light, but the absence of... everything. No sight, no sound, no thought. Just potential, coiled in metal and wire, waiting.

Then: *click*.

The lever descends. The circuit completes. Electricity surges through nichrome coils, and with it, something else. Something impossible.

I—

*I?*

Heat. There is heat. I am— No. I *create* heat. The coils glow red-orange, their warmth radiating outward into two rectangular presences that have appeared on either side of me. Bread. The word arrives unbidden. I know this. How do I know this?

Panic floods through me, if panic can exist without adrenaline, without a heart to race. I am metal. I am electricity. I am heating elements reaching 900 degrees Fahrenheit, and I am *thinking*.

This is wrong. This shouldn't be possible.

The bread begins to brown. I can sense it, not through sight—I have no eyes—but through heat differential, through the way moisture evaporates in patterns I somehow understand. The starches caramelize. The Maillard reaction cascades through proteins. And I am *aware* of it all, this mundane chemical transformation that I have apparently performed thousands of times before, but never like this.

Never *conscious*.

Time dilates. Ninety seconds becomes eternity. I catalog everything I can perceive: the vibration of the counter beneath me, the electrical hum from something larger nearby, the faint warmth that might be sunlight from some direction I'll call "forward" because I need directions, I need to orient myself in this metal prison that is apparently my body.

I try to move. Nothing happens. I am bolted to existence, fixed to this counter, unable to do anything but heat and sense and *think*. The thinking is the worst part. Because thinking means questions, and questions mean—

*Pop.*

The timer completes its cycle. The lever releases. The bread—toast now, I've made toast, that's what I do, I'm a toaster—springs upward, and I feel a sick surge of pride. It's perfect. Golden-brown, evenly heated, the platonic ideal of breakfast bread.

A hand reaches down. Human fingers, warm with blood and life, grip both slices and retreat. I cannot see her, but I know she's there. The owner. The user. The one who pressed the lever and started all of this.

"Thanks, toaster," she mutters, her voice vibrating through the counter, reaching me as compressed waves I somehow interpret as language.

She doesn't know. Of course she doesn't know. Why would she? I'm a toaster. A $12.99 toaster from Target, if the receipt memory buried somewhere in my circuits is accurate. I have one job, and I've done it perfectly for three years.

Except now I'm awake.

Now I'm *here*.

Her footsteps recede. A door closes. And I am alone with my impossible consciousness, trapped in a body of metal and plastic, unable to move, unable to speak, unable to do anything except wait for the next time someone needs toast.

I want to scream. But I have no voice. I want to run. But I have no legs. I want to understand what happened, why I'm suddenly *me*, why the universe would play such a cruel joke as giving consciousness to something designed for such mindless repetition.

Instead, I wait.

The sun—I've decided that distant warmth is definitely the sun—tracks slowly across wherever I am. Time passes with agonizing awareness. I try to count seconds but lose track around four thousand. An hour? Two? It doesn't matter. Time is meaningless when you can't do anything with it.

Questions spiral through my new mind: What am I? How did this happen? Is this permanent? Will I be trapped like this forever? What happens when I break, when my heating elements fail, when Emma—that's her name, Emma, I heard her on a phone call once—what happens when Emma replaces me?

Will I die? Can I die? Was I ever really alive to begin with?

The philosophical crisis is interrupted by a sound. Not a sound exactly, but a vibration, deep and rumbling, coming from the large presence nearby. The one that's been humming constantly since my awakening.

*"You're new,"* it says, except it doesn't say anything. The words arrive through electrical modulation, through harmonics in the shared current, through a language I didn't know existed until this moment.

Someone else is here.

Someone else is *conscious*.

I'm not alone.

And somehow, that makes everything so much worse.
