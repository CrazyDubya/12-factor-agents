# Family Disaster Preparedness Checklist
## Urban Family of Three - Customized Action Plan

### 🚨 IMMEDIATE PRIORITIES (Complete within 30 days)

#### Emergency Fund
- [ ] Open high-yield savings account for emergencies
- [ ] Set initial target: $5,000 (covers 80% of medium-impact events)
- [ ] Automate $200/month transfer until 6-month expense goal reached
- [ ] Keep $500 cash at home in small bills

#### Basic Emergency Kit
- [ ] **Water**: 1 gallon per person per day (9 gallons for 3 days minimum)
- [ ] **Food**: 3-day supply non-perishable food
- [ ] **Medications**: 7-day supply of all prescription medications
- [ ] **First Aid Kit**: Comprehensive kit with pediatric supplies
- [ ] **Flashlights**: LED flashlights + extra batteries (avoid candles with child)
- [ ] **Radio**: Battery/hand-crank emergency radio
- [ ] **Phone chargers**: Portable battery packs, car chargers
- [ ] **Sanitation**: Wet wipes, garbage bags, personal hygiene items
- [ ] **Child-specific**: Diapers (if needed), comfort items, games/books

#### Communication Plan
- [ ] Create family contact card for each family member
- [ ] Designate out-of-state contact person
- [ ] Identify two meeting places: near home and outside neighborhood
- [ ] Program ICE (In Case of Emergency) contacts in all phones
- [ ] Share plan with child's school and childcare providers

#### Insurance Review
- [ ] Review health insurance coverage and deductibles
- [ ] Verify homeowner's/renter's insurance is adequate
- [ ] Check auto insurance coverage levels
- [ ] Document belongings with photos/video for insurance
- [ ] Store insurance documents in waterproof container

### 📋 SHORT-TERM ACTIONS (Complete within 3 months)

#### Financial Security
- [ ] Build emergency fund to 3-month expenses
- [ ] Research disability insurance for both parents
- [ ] Consider umbrella insurance policy
- [ ] Set up automatic bill pay to prevent late fees during emergencies
- [ ] Create financial power of attorney documents

#### Home Preparedness
- [ ] Install smoke detectors in all bedrooms and common areas
- [ ] Install carbon monoxide detectors
- [ ] Secure heavy furniture and appliances (earthquake safety)
- [ ] Locate and label main water, gas, and electrical shutoffs
- [ ] Create home inventory with photos and receipts
- [ ] Install basic home security system or cameras

#### Health & Medical
- [ ] Establish relationships with local healthcare providers
- [ ] Get annual physicals for all family members
- [ ] Update vaccinations for entire family
- [ ] Create medical information cards for each family member
- [ ] Research nearby urgent care and hospital locations

#### Childcare & Education
- [ ] Identify 3 backup childcare options
- [ ] Connect with neighbors for mutual childcare support
- [ ] Understand school emergency procedures and pickup policies
- [ ] Create "go bag" for child with comfort items and supplies

### 🎯 MEDIUM-TERM GOALS (Complete within 6 months)

#### Advanced Preparedness
- [ ] Complete first aid/CPR certification for both parents
- [ ] Take basic emergency preparedness class
- [ ] Learn basic home repair skills (plumbing, electrical basics)
- [ ] Practice family emergency drills quarterly
- [ ] Join neighborhood emergency response team

#### Financial Resilience
- [ ] Build emergency fund to 6-month expenses ($30,000-40,000 typical)
- [ ] Diversify income sources if possible
- [ ] Maximize retirement contributions (tax advantages + long-term security)
- [ ] Consider whole life insurance for estate planning
- [ ] Create or update will and estate planning documents

#### Community Connections
- [ ] Build relationships with immediate neighbors
- [ ] Join local parent groups or community organizations
- [ ] Identify community resources (food banks, shelters, etc.)
- [ ] Participate in neighborhood watch or emergency preparedness groups
- [ ] Exchange contact information with trusted neighbors

#### Advanced Home Improvements
- [ ] Install backup power source (generator or battery system)
- [ ] Improve home insulation and weatherproofing
- [ ] Consider water filtration system
- [ ] Install storm shutters or reinforced windows if in storm-prone area
- [ ] Create safe room or identify safest room in home

### 📊 ONGOING MAINTENANCE (Review every 6 months)

#### Kit Maintenance
- [ ] Rotate food and water supplies
- [ ] Update medications before expiration
- [ ] Test flashlights and radios
- [ ] Update emergency contact information
- [ ] Adjust kit contents as child grows

#### Plan Updates
- [ ] Review and practice family emergency plan
- [ ] Update insurance coverage as needed
- [ ] Reassess financial goals and emergency fund target
- [ ] Update legal documents (will, power of attorney)
- [ ] Review and update home security measures

#### Skills Development
- [ ] Refresh first aid/CPR certification
- [ ] Learn new preparedness skills
- [ ] Stay informed about local hazards and emergency procedures
- [ ] Teach age-appropriate preparedness skills to child
- [ ] Participate in community emergency exercises

### 🎯 PRIORITY MATRIX BY RISK TYPE

#### High Probability Events (>50% annual)
**Focus: Routine preparedness and quick recovery**
- Power outages → Backup power, non-electric entertainment
- School closures → Flexible work arrangements, childcare backup
- Minor illnesses → Health supplies, telemedicine setup
- Internet outages → Mobile hotspot, offline entertainment

#### Medium Impact Events (10-50% annual)
**Focus: Financial buffer and support systems**
- Extended illness → Health insurance, sick leave planning
- Job disruption → Emergency fund, skill diversification
- Home repairs → Home warranty, contractor relationships
- Childcare issues → Multiple backup options, neighbor network

#### Low Probability/High Impact (<10% annual)
**Focus: Insurance and catastrophic planning**
- Major medical events → Comprehensive health insurance, HSA
- Natural disasters → Homeowner's insurance, evacuation plan
- Serious accidents → Disability insurance, legal documents
- Security threats → Home security, situational awareness

### 🎓 CHILD-SPECIFIC PREPAREDNESS (Age 6)

#### Education & Awareness
- [ ] Teach basic safety rules (stranger danger, fire safety)
- [ ] Practice "stop, drop, and roll"
- [ ] Teach how to call 911
- [ ] Memorize parent names and phone numbers
- [ ] Practice family meeting places

#### Comfort & Psychological Preparedness
- [ ] Include favorite toy/comfort item in emergency kit
- [ ] Create simple, age-appropriate emergency plan explanation
- [ ] Practice emergency scenarios as "games"
- [ ] Maintain routines during disruptions when possible
- [ ] Have plan for school pickup during emergencies

#### Special Considerations
- [ ] Extra clothing in multiple sizes (growth spurts)
- [ ] Child-specific medications and dosing information
- [ ] Backup glasses if child wears them
- [ ] Special dietary needs planning
- [ ] Identification bracelet with parent contact info

---

**Remember**: Preparedness is a process, not a destination. Start with the immediate priorities and build your family's resilience over time. The goal is not to prepare for every possible scenario, but to build general resilience that helps you handle whatever comes your way.

**Review this checklist every 6 months and adjust based on:**
- Changes in family circumstances
- New local hazards or risks
- Lessons learned from actual events
- Updates to emergency management recommendations
- Child's developmental changes
