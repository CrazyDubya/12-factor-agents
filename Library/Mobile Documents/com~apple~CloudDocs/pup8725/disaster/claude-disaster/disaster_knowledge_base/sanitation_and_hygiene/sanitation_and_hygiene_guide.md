# Urban Sanitation & Hygiene Guide

In a disaster, the failure of water and sanitation systems can lead to the rapid spread of disease. This guide covers the essentials of maintaining sanitation and hygiene to keep your family healthy.

## 1. Emergency Toilets

When the sewer system is down, you must have a way to safely dispose of human waste.

### The Twin-Bucket Toilet System

This is the simplest and most effective solution.

*   **You Will Need:**
    *   Two 5-gallon buckets with tight-fitting lids.
    *   A toilet seat that can snap onto a bucket (optional, but recommended for comfort).
    *   Carbon material: Sawdust, wood shavings, shredded newspaper, or even dry leaves.
    *   Heavy-duty trash bags.

*   **Setup and Use:**
    1.  **Bucket #1 (Urine):** This bucket is for urine only. Urine is sterile and can be safely poured out in a designated spot away from your main living area.
    2.  **Bucket #2 (Feces):**
        *   Line the bucket with a trash bag.
        *   After each use, completely cover the waste with a generous layer of your carbon material. This is the most important step. The carbon material helps to absorb moisture, reduce odor, and begin the composting process.
        *   Keep the lid on tight when not in use.
    3.  **Disposal:** When the bucket is full, tie off the bag securely. Store it in a designated, secure area away from your living space until normal waste collection services resume.

**IMPORTANT:** Never mix urine and feces. This is what creates dangerous pathogens and strong odors.

## 2. Personal Hygiene

Staying clean is crucial for preventing skin infections and other illnesses.

*   **Handwashing:**
    *   Hand sanitizer (at least 60% alcohol) is a good option when water is scarce.
    *   You can create a simple handwashing station with a water container with a spigot, a bucket to catch the runoff, and soap.
*   **Bathing:**
    *   A "sponge bath" with a washcloth and a small amount of water is very effective.
    *   Unscented baby wipes are excellent for quick clean-ups.
*   **Dental Care:** Continue to brush your teeth. Use bottled or purified water.

## 3. Garbage and Waste Disposal

Unmanaged trash will attract pests and spread disease.

*   **Separate Your Waste:** Separate wet food scraps from dry trash.
*   **Secure Storage:** Store all garbage in sturdy bags in containers with tight-fitting lids. If possible, keep the containers in a garage or shed, away from your main living area.
*   **Burying (If Necessary):** If waste collection is suspended for a long time, you may need to bury your garbage. Choose a location that is at least 200 feet away from any water source.

By managing waste and maintaining personal hygiene, you can create a healthier and safer environment for your family during a prolonged urban disaster.
