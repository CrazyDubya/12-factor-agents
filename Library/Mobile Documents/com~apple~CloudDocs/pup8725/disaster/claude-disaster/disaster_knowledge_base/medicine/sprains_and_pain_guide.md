# First Aid for Sprains and Basic Pains

This guide covers the immediate first aid for sprains and the management of common aches and pains.

## 1. Treating Sprains: The R.I.C.E. Method

A sprain is an injury to a ligament (the tissue that connects bones to other bones). The most common location for a sprain is the ankle. For the first 24-48 hours after an injury, follow the R.I.C.E. method to reduce pain and swelling.

*   **Rest:** Stop all activity and rest the injured joint. Do not put weight on it.
*   **Ice:** Apply a cold pack (a bag of frozen vegetables works well) to the injured area for 15-20 minutes at a time, every 2-4 hours. Wrap the cold pack in a towel to prevent frostbite.
*   **Compression:** Wrap the injured area with an elastic bandage (like an ACE bandage). It should be snug, but not so tight that it cuts off circulation.
*   **Elevation:** Keep the injured limb elevated above the level of your heart as much as possible.

### Things to Avoid for the First 48 Hours (H.A.R.M.)

*   **Heat:** Avoid hot showers, heat packs, or saunas.
*   **Alcohol:** Alcohol can increase swelling and mask pain, which can lead to further injury.
*   **Running:** Or any other form of strenuous exercise.
*   **Massage:** Can increase swelling and bleeding in the injured tissue.

## 2. Managing Basic Pain

For common aches and pains (headaches, muscle soreness, etc.), over-the-counter pain relievers can be effective. **Always read the label and do not exceed the recommended dose.**

### Common Over-the-Counter Options:

*   **Acetaminophen (Tylenol):** A good choice for general pain relief.
*   **Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin):** An anti-inflammatory, which makes it particularly good for sprains and other injuries where swelling is a factor.
*   **Naproxen (Aleve):** Another anti-inflammatory, similar to ibuprofen.

**Important Note:** Do not give aspirin to children or teenagers, as it has been linked to a rare but serious condition called Reye's syndrome.

## When to Seek Medical Help

This guide is for minor injuries. You should seek professional medical help if:

*   You suspect a bone is broken.
*   The pain is severe and does not improve with over-the-counter medication.
*   The swelling does not go down after 48 hours.
*   You cannot put any weight on the injured limb.
*   The joint feels unstable or "wobbly."
