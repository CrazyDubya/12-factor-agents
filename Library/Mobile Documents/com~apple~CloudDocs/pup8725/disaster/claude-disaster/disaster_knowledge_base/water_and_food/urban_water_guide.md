# Urban Water Procurement and Purification Guide

In a disaster, clean drinking water is your most immediate priority. This guide outlines how to secure and purify water in an urban environment.

## 1. Storing Emergency Water

The easiest and safest water is what you store *before* a disaster.

*   **How Much:** Store a **minimum** of **1 gallon (4 liters) per person, per day**. Aim for at least a 3-day supply, with a 2-week supply being ideal. Don't forget to account for pets.
*   **Best Containers:**
    *   Commercially bottled water (easiest, safest).
    *   Food-grade water storage containers (available at camping or surplus stores).
    *   Thoroughly cleaned and sanitized 2-liter soda bottles or other sturdy, sealable containers.
*   **Sanitizing Your Own Containers:**
    1.  Wash the container and rinse thoroughly.
    2.  Mix 1 teaspoon of unscented liquid household bleach with a quart of water.
    3.  Pour the bleach solution into your container, close it, and shake well so it touches all interior surfaces.
    4.  Rinse thoroughly with clean water.
*   **Storage Location:** Store water in a cool, dark place. Keep plastic containers away from gasoline, pesticides, or other chemical fumes, as vapors can penetrate the plastic over time.

## 2. Finding Water Sources in an Urban Environment

If your stored water runs out, you can find water in several places within your home.

*   **Hot Water Heater Tank:** A typical tank holds 30-50 gallons. Turn off the power or gas supply first, then open the drain valve at the bottom.
*   **Toilet Tank (NOT the bowl):** The water in the upper tank is clean (unless you use chemical cleaners).
*   **Pipes:** There will be water trapped in your home's plumbing. You can drain it from the lowest point in the house.
*   **Ice Cubes:** Melt any ice you have in the freezer.
*   **Canned Goods:** The liquid from canned fruits and vegetables is safe to drink.

**External Sources (Use with Extreme Caution):**
*   Rainwater (collect from downspouts)
*   Rivers, streams, or ponds

**!! DANGER !! - DO NOT DRINK WATER FROM:**
*   Radiators or hot water boilers used for home heating.
*   Waterbeds (often treated with chemicals).
*   Swimming pools or spas (high chemical content).
*   Any water you suspect is contaminated with fuel, fire-fighting foam, or other toxic chemicals.

## 3. How to Purify Water

If you use water from any source other than your own clean, stored containers, you **MUST** purify it.

### Step 1: Pre-Filter (If Necessary)

If the water is cloudy or has debris, you must filter it first.
*   Pour the water through a coffee filter, a clean cloth (like a t-shirt), or several layers of paper towels.

### Step 2: Purify

Choose one of the following methods:

*   **Boiling (Most Effective):**
    1.  Bring the water to a rolling boil.
    2.  Let it boil for **at least 1 full minute**. (At altitudes above 5,000 ft / 1,500m, boil for 3 minutes).
    3.  Let the water cool before drinking.

*   **Chemical Disinfection (Bleach):**
    *Use only regular, unscented household bleach (5.25% sodium hypochlorite).*
    1.  Place the water in a clean container.
    2.  Add 16 drops of bleach per gallon of water (or 8 drops per 2-liter bottle).
    3.  Stir or shake the container.
    4.  Let it stand for **30 minutes**.
    5.  The water should have a faint chlorine smell. If it doesn't, repeat the dosage and let it stand for another 15 minutes.

*   **Water Purification Tablets:**
    *   Follow the manufacturer's instructions precisely. These are a great item for a bug-out bag.

*   **Portable Water Filters:**
    *   Many commercial camping filters are very effective at removing bacteria and protozoa.
    *   **IMPORTANT:** Most portable filters do **not** remove viruses. It is often recommended to disinfect the water with bleach or boiling *after* filtering.

By following these steps, you can ensure you have a safe supply of drinking water during an urban disaster.
