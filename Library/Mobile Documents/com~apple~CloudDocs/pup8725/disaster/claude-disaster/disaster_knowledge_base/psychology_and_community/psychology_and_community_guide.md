# Survival Psychology & Community Guide

This guide covers the mental aspects of survival and the practical steps for building a community to weather a crisis.

## 1. The Psychology of Survival: The Rule of Threes

The most important survival tool is your mind. A common saying in survival training is the "Rule of Threes":

*   You can survive **3 minutes** without air.
*   You can survive **3 hours** without shelter in a harsh environment.
*   You can survive **3 days** without water.
*   You can survive **3 weeks** without food.

This rule highlights the priorities. But underlying all of it is the will to live. Without a positive mental attitude and the will to survive, all the skills and supplies in the world are useless.

### Key Mental States to Cultivate:

*   **Acceptance:** Accept the reality of the situation. Denial is a dangerous waste of time.
*   **Positivity:** Maintain a positive but realistic outlook. Hope is a powerful motivator.
*   **Focus:** Concentrate on what you can do *right now* to improve your situation. Don't get lost in long-term worries.
*   **Calmness:** Panic leads to bad decisions. When you feel overwhelmed, stop, take a deep breath, and think before you act.

## 2. Mutual Assistance Groups (MAGs)

In a long-term urban disaster, the lone wolf is a myth. The single most important factor for survival is a strong, cohesive group. A Mutual Assistance Group (MAG) is a pre-planned community of like-minded individuals who agree to pool their resources and skills in an emergency.

### Why Form a MAG?

*   **Security:** There is safety in numbers. A group is a much harder target for desperate people.
*   **Shared Resources:** No one person can have all the skills and supplies needed. A group allows for specialization (e.g., one person is a medic, another is a mechanic, another is a gardener).
*   **Shared Workload:** Many hands make light work. A group can share the burden of tasks like standing watch, purifying water, and caring for the sick.
*   **Psychological Support:** A group provides a sense of belonging and shared purpose, which is critical for long-term morale.

### How to Form a MAG:

1.  **Start with Your Inner Circle:** Begin with trusted family and close friends.
2.  **Expand to Your Neighborhood:** Get to know your neighbors *before* a disaster. Identify those who are reliable and have useful skills.
3.  **Discuss Expectations:** Have an open and honest conversation about what each member is expected to contribute. This includes skills, supplies, and time.
4.  **Create a Plan:** Develop a simple, written plan that outlines your group's goals, communication methods, and meeting places.
5.  **Practice Together:** Conduct drills and practice your plan. This will build trust and identify weaknesses in your plan before a real emergency.

Building a strong community is the ultimate preparedness strategy. Start today by getting to know your neighbors.
