# ☢️ Radiation Detection Equipment Guide

## 🎯 Overview
Radiation detection is critical for nuclear emergency response. You cannot see, smell, taste, or feel ionizing radiation - only proper instruments can detect it. This guide covers essential detection equipment for family preparedness.

## 📟 Types of Radiation Detectors

### **Geiger Counter (Survey Meter)**
**Best for: General radiation detection and monitoring**

#### **How It Works**
- Detects gamma and beta radiation
- Produces audible clicks for radiation events
- Displays readings in counts per minute (CPM) or dose rate

#### **Recommended Models**
| Model | Price | Sensitivity | Best For |
|-------|-------|-------------|----------|
| RadAlert 100 | $150 | High | Basic monitoring |
| Inspector Alert | $600 | Very High | Alpha/beta/gamma |
| Digilert 100 | $350 | High | Digital accuracy |

### **Dosimeter**
**Best for: Personal radiation exposure tracking**

#### **Types**
- **Film badge** - Develops based on exposure
- **TLD** - Thermoluminescent dosimeter  
- **Electronic** - Real-time dose display
- **Pen dosimeter** - Pocket-sized, immediate reading

#### **Key Features**
- Measures cumulative dose over time
- Shows total exposure in mSv or mrem
- Essential for tracking safe exposure limits

### **Radiation Spectrometer**
**Best for: Identifying specific radioactive materials**

#### **Capabilities**
- Identifies gamma-emitting isotopes
- Shows energy spectrum
- Helps determine contamination source
- Professional-grade analysis

## 📊 Understanding Readings

### **Radiation Units**
| Unit | Measures | Conversion |
|------|----------|------------|
| **CPM** | Counts per minute | Detector clicks |
| **mR/hr** | Milliroentgen per hour | Exposure rate |
| **μSv/hr** | Microsievert per hour | Dose rate |
| **mSv** | Millisievert | Cumulative dose |

#### **Conversion Factors**
- 1 mR/hr ≈ 10 μSv/hr (rough conversion)
- 1000 μSv = 1 mSv
- Background: 10-20 μSv/hr normal
- Concern level: 100+ μSv/hr

### **Action Levels**
| Reading | Action Required |
|---------|----------------|
| **0-50 μSv/hr** | Normal background |
| **50-100 μSv/hr** | Monitor, investigate source |
| **100-1000 μSv/hr** | Limit time, increase distance |
| **1000+ μSv/hr** | Evacuate area immediately |
| **10,000+ μSv/hr** | Extreme danger - flee |

## 🔧 Operation Basics

### **Before First Use**
1. **Read manual thoroughly** - Each detector is different
2. **Install fresh batteries** - Check voltage requirements  
3. **Perform background check** - Establish normal readings
4. **Test with check source** - Verify proper operation
5. **Practice operation** - Become familiar with controls

### **Taking Readings**
1. **Turn on detector** - Allow warm-up time if required
2. **Check battery** - Low battery gives false readings
3. **Hold properly** - Chest height, steady position
4. **Wait for stability** - Readings may fluctuate initially
5. **Record results** - Date, time, location, reading

### **Contamination Checking**
- **1 inch from surface** - Standard measurement distance
- **Systematic survey** - Cover all surfaces methodically  
- **Compare to background** - 2x background = possible contamination
- **Multiple readings** - Confirm elevated readings
- **Mark contaminated areas** - Prevent spread

## ⚠️ Important Limitations

### **What Detectors CAN Do**
✅ Detect presence of gamma/beta radiation  
✅ Measure dose rates and exposure  
✅ Alert to dangerous levels  
✅ Track contamination spread  
✅ Monitor personal exposure  

### **What Detectors CANNOT Do**
❌ Detect alpha radiation (most units)  
❌ Identify specific isotopes (basic units)  
❌ Predict health effects  
❌ Remove contamination  
❌ Provide medical treatment  

### **False Readings**
- **Low battery** - May read high or low
- **Temperature** - Extreme heat/cold affects readings
- **Humidity** - Can cause erratic readings
- **Medical isotopes** - Patients with medical scans
- **Smoke detectors** - Contain small radioactive sources

## 🛡️ Safety Principles

### **Time, Distance, Shielding**
- **TIME** - Limit exposure duration
- **DISTANCE** - Double distance = 1/4 radiation
- **SHIELDING** - Dense materials block radiation

### **ALARA Principle**
**As Low As Reasonably Achievable**
- Even small doses have some risk
- Minimize unnecessary exposure
- Use detection to guide safety decisions

## 💡 Pro Tips

### **Maintenance**
- **Monthly battery check** - Replace before low
- **Annual calibration** - Professional calibration recommended
- **Clean regularly** - Dust affects sensitive instruments
- **Store properly** - Dry, temperature-controlled environment

### **Training**
- **Practice regularly** - Familiarity saves time in emergency
- **Know your normal** - Establish baseline readings
- **Understand units** - Don't get confused by different scales
- **Emergency contacts** - Know who to call with readings

### **Cost Savings**
- **Used equipment** - Check calibration date
- **Group purchase** - Split costs with neighbors
- **Rental options** - For short-term needs
- **Emergency services** - May provide detection in crisis

## 📞 When to Call for Help

### **Professional Assessment Needed**
- Readings above 1000 μSv/hr
- Contamination that won't decontaminate
- Exposure above annual limits
- Instrument malfunction during emergency
- Uncertainty about readings

### **Emergency Contacts**
- **911** - Life-threatening exposure
- **Poison Control** - 1-800-222-1222
- **State Radiation Control** - Each state has program
- **EPA Radiological Emergency** - Federal response
